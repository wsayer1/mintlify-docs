title: "Capacity, waitlists, and visibility"
description: "Control who can find your event and how many people can attend."
---

Two settings shape who ends up at your event: **visibility** (who can find it) and **capacity** (how many can attend).

## Public vs. private

|  | Public | Private |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appears on Explore | Yes | No |
| Reachable via the event link | Yes | Yes |
| Guests can register | Yes | Yes |

Use **public** for meetups, talks, and workshops you want anyone to discover. Use **private** for invite-only gatherings like founder dinners — the event page shows a **Private event** badge, and only people with the link can find it.

You can switch visibility anytime by editing the event. Making a public event private removes it from Explore but doesn't affect guests who already registered.

## Capacity and the waitlist

Set a capacity to cap confirmed guests at that number. Everything past that is handled automatically:

1. Registrations up to the cap are confirmed (**Going**).
2. Once full, new registrations are **Waitlisted**, in the order they arrive.
3. When a confirmed guest cancels, the first waitlisted guest is promoted to **Going** automatically.

You'll see both numbers — confirmed and waitlisted — on your [Dashboard](/hosts/dashboard) and on the event's manage page.

<Tip>
  Raising the capacity later? Newly opened spots aren't backfilled from the waitlist retroactively, but the next registrations will be confirmed until the new cap is reached. Waitlisted guests are still promoted whenever someone cancels.
</Tip>

Leave capacity empty for unlimited attendance — every registration is confirmed instantly.

## Online events and meeting links

For online events, the meeting link you enter is treated as confidential:

- **Confirmed guests** see the link on the event page.
- **Waitlisted guests, unregistered visitors, and signed-out visitors** see only that it's an online event, with a note that the link is revealed after registering.
- **You**, as the host, always see it.

This means you can share your event page freely — even publicly — without exposing the call to uninvited drop-ins.
